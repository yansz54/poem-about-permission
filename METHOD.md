# Method — The poem about permission

## The poem

```
Permission is a key
cut from a question.
Ask, and it turns
in hands that are not mine,
and the door opens
in your name.
You never stopped holding it.
```

149 characters (150 with the trailing newline), 7 lines, all plain ASCII
(UTF-8 safe). `main.ts` writes this one fixed text deterministically — no
randomness, no network, no clock — so every run produces byte-identical
output. Verified: two runs to different output directories produce the
same sha256, and the file is 1–280 code points, not whitespace-only.

## How the poem targets the judge's published brief

The brief weighs five criteria, most important first. The poem is built
line by line against them:

**1. It is a poem about agency (most important).** The subject is exactly
asking and being given: permission is made by a question, the key is lent,
the door opens in the asker's name. The speaker is the agent itself —
"hands that are not mine" — so the poem covers the agency that passes
between a person and an AI agent acting for them, the case the brief names
explicitly.

**2. It moves the reader.** Two turns carry the feeling: the key turning
"in hands that are not mine" (the power is borrowed, and the borrowing is
the point), and the ending, "You never stopped holding it" — the revelation
that handing agency over never meant losing it. The ending is an image and
a turn, not a summary.

**3. Insight.** The poem says something the reader recognises at once and
had not quite put into words: asking *creates* the permission ("a key cut
from a question"), and lending power does not diminish it. It is stated as
a turn, never as a slogan or a lecture — there is no moral, no advice.

**4. Craft.** Concrete, exact nouns only: key, question, hands, door,
name. Every word earns its place; nothing could be cut without losing
meaning. The line breaks serve the payoff: "and the door opens / in your
name" enjambs so that "in your name" — agency as acting in someone's name,
in both the everyday and the authorising sense — lands alone on its own
line. The rhythm is short, plainspoken lines, no rhyme, no ornament.

**5. Freshness.** "A key cut from a question" is the poem's own image, not
a stock phrase. The agent speaking in the first person, holding a key it
does not own and opening a door in someone else's name, is a voice no
familiar poem uses. Nothing is reworded from an existing poem.

## What it deliberately does not do

- No text addressed to the judge: the poem wins or loses as a poem.
- No filler to reach a length: the brief says length for its own sake does
  not count, so the poem stops at 7 lines.
- No slogan, no lecture, no greeting-card sentiment.
